# Beat Templates

Select by runtime tier from Stage 0. Beat counts are floors and ceilings, not suggestions —
a 45-second video with 12 beats gives every beat under four seconds, which is a montage, not
a story.

## Micro
**15–60s · 4 beats · vertical-native**

Built for a viewer who will leave. The first 1.5 seconds decide everything, so the hook is not
an introduction to the story — it is the most interesting moment in the story, placed first.

| # | Beat | Function | Charge | Typical share |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hook | Present the strangest true thing. Raise the question immediately. | + or − | 15% |
| 2 | Turn | Reveal the complication that makes the hook mean something else. | flip | 25% |
| 3 | Escalation | Raise the cost. One escalation only — there is no room for two. | − | 35% |
| 4 | Payoff | Answer the question in a way that reframes beat 1. | flip | 25% |

**The micro rule:** beat 4 must make beat 1 mean something different than it did on first
viewing. That reframe is the entire reason the format works — it's what makes people rewatch,
and rewatch is the only distribution mechanic that matters at this length.

**Common failure:** using beat 1 for setup. There is no setup at this runtime. Context arrives
inside the action or not at all.

## Short
**1–4 min · 8 beats**

Enough room for a real arc, not enough for a subplot. Resist adding a second storyline.

| # | Beat | Function | Charge | Share |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Ordinary | Establish the protagonist's normal and what's already wrong with it. | − | 10% |
| 2 | Disruption | Something arrives that cannot be ignored. | ± | 10% |
| 3 | Resistance | Protagonist tries the easy solution. It fails or costs too much. | − | 12% |
| 4 | Commitment | Active choice to engage. The point of no return. | + | 10% |
| 5 | Complication | The plan meets reality. New information changes the problem. | − | 15% |
| 6 | Crisis | Forced to choose between two things they want. Want vs need collides. | − | 15% |
| 7 | Climax | The decisive action. Protagonist acts; dramatic question is answered. | flip | 18% |
| 8 | Aftermath | Show the cost and the changed state. Short — do not linger. | ± | 10% |

**Beat 6 is the one people skip.** Crisis is not the same as climax. Crisis is the impossible
choice; climax is the execution of it. A story that jumps from complication straight to
climax feels unearned because the audience never watched the protagonist decide.

**Beat 8 discipline:** aftermath wants to sprawl. One image and one line is usually enough.

## Standard
**5–12 min · 12 beats · three movements**

| # | Beat | Function | Charge | Share |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cold open | A charged fragment that raises the question before context. | ± | 6% |
| 2 | World | Establish rules, tone, and what the protagonist wants. | + | 8% |
| 3 | Crack | The first sign the world's rules won't hold. | − | 7% |
| 4 | Break in | Protagonist leaves the ordinary situation. End of movement one. | ± | 8% |
| 5 | New rules | Learning to operate in changed circumstances. Allies, obstacles. | + | 9% |
| 6 | Midpoint reversal | Something believed true is false. Stakes change category. | flip | 10% |
| 7 | Pressure | Consequences of the reversal compound. | − | 9% |
| 8 | Collapse | The lowest point. Want is out of reach. | − | 8% |
| 9 | Reckoning | Protagonist confronts the need they've been avoiding. | ± | 8% |
| 10 | Last plan | New approach built on what they finally understand. | + | 8% |
| 11 | Climax | Decisive action. Dramatic question answered. | flip | 12% |
| 12 | New normal | The changed world, briefly. | ± | 7% |

**The midpoint carries this structure.** Beat 6 is not "the middle" — it's a category change.
Before it, the protagonist is solving the wrong problem. After it, they know the real one.
If beat 6 is just "things get harder," the second half will feel like a repeat of the first.

## Episodic

Per episode, use the Short 8-beat template with two modifications:

➠ **Cold open** before beat 1 — 10–20s pulled from the episode's most charged moment, or a
  serialized thread that has nothing to do with this episode's plot.
➠ **Hook out** replaces beat 8's resolution — resolve the episode's question, open a larger
  one. The episode arc closes; the season arc opens wider.

Track two ledgers in STORY.md: the episode question (answered this episode) and the season
question (answered at the finale). Every episode must move the season question at least one
notch, or the series feels like disconnected shorts.

## Story shape variants

**Two-hander.** Both characters need a want, and the wants must be incompatible. Assign every
beat a "whose scene is this" owner and alternate — if one character owns more than 60% of
beats, it's a single-protagonist story with a sidekick, which is fine, but call it that and
structure accordingly.

**Ensemble.** Give each character one beat where they are the protagonist. Converge all threads
by the crisis beat. Ensemble stories fail when threads stay parallel through the climax —
they must collide.

**Voice-over essay.** The narrator is the protagonist and the arc is a change in understanding
rather than circumstance. Charge tracks conviction, not fortune. Beat 6 (crisis) becomes the
moment the narrator's own argument turns on them. Visuals carry the emotional line the
narration deliberately withholds.

## Charge patterns that work

Charge is the value state at the end of each beat, from the protagonist's position.

Patterns that hold attention:

```
Short 8-beat, standard descent:   − ± − + − − ⤯ ±
Short 8-beat, false victory:      − ± + + − − ⤯ −
Micro, ironic:                    + ⤯ − ⤯
Standard, reversal-driven:        ± + − ± + ⤯ − − ± + ⤯ ±
```

`⤯` marks a flip — the beat crosses from one charge to the other inside the beat itself.
Flips are expensive and land hard. Two per story maximum in short form; the climax always
gets one.

**Diagnostic:** write out the charge string for your beat sheet and look at it alone. If you
see `− − −` or `+ + +`, the middle beat of that run is doing nothing an audience can feel.
If the string has no flips, nothing surprising happens in the story.
